cutline — method

Data

Every UEFA club competition match since July 2021 (Champions League, Europa League, Conference League, qualifiers included), from UEFA's public match data. Every top-division match in 18 European leagues from football-data.co.uk, with closing bookmaker odds. About 32,000 matches. Clubs from leagues without domestic data (Azerbaijan, Czechia, Slovakia, Ukraine) are rated from their European matches only.

Rating clubs

Each club has an attack and a defence strength. The expected goals of a team are exp(league goal level + home advantage + its attack − the opponent's defence). Goals are Poisson with the Dixon–Coles correction for low scores, which makes 0–0 and 1–1 slightly more likely than two independent Poissons would.

Domestic leagues each have their own goal level; European matches link the leagues together. A club's strength is its country's level plus its own deviation, so a club with few matches is pulled towards its country rather than towards the European average. Recent matches count more: a match a year old has half the weight of one played today.

The fit also gives each strength a standard error. Every simulated season draws its own strengths from that uncertainty, so the probabilities include "we don't know exactly how good this club is", not only match luck.

Simulating

For each of 10,000 seasons: draw club strengths, sample a score for every unplayed league-phase match, rank the table with UEFA's tiebreakers (points, goal difference, goals, away goals, wins, away wins, then opponents' points, goal difference and goals), then play the knockout phase: the play-off, the seeded bracket, two legs with extra time and penalties, and a neutral final. The table code reproduces the official 2024-25 and 2025-26 final tables exactly from the results.

"What if" re-runs the same 10,000 seasons in your browser with the results you set fixed. It uses the same seed as the published snapshot, so with nothing set it gives exactly the published numbers.

Testing it

Settings were chosen on the 2023-24 season only, pinned in the code, and then the model was scored once on 2024-25 and 2025-26 against an Elo baseline and bookmaker closing odds. Results (full report):

On 558 Champions League matches the model's ranked probability score was 0.197, against 0.217 for Elo and 0.235 for league base rates (lower is better), and it was ahead in both seasons. On 10,231 domestic matches it was only marginally better than Elo (0.205 vs 0.205) and the betting market beat both (0.198): closing odds know about injuries and line-ups, the model does not.

For whole-season questions, simulated before each league-phase matchday of both seasons, the model had the best Brier score for "finishes top 8" (0.103 vs Elo 0.127) and "reaches the quarter-finals" (0.111 vs 0.132). Elo was slightly better at the 24th-place cut (0.101 vs 0.105). For the eventual winner nobody beat treating every club as equal; both winners came from 15th and 11th in the league phase. Two seasons is a small sample.

Limits

No injuries, suspensions or transfers: the model only sees results. Penalty shoot-outs are 50/50. Two seasons in this format is a small sample for checking whether "60%" really happens 60% of the time.
